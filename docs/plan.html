Task scenarios

This is the wording for the no-guide document. The guide version adds the expected result, the numbered steps written against the final button labels, and one line on how the prototype implements the scenario, which the rubric's "fully explained" asks for. Together they cover all four features in the research report, and Scenario 3 shows school and the rest of life ranked together. Scenario 1 is the lo-fi scenario, as the Day 17 slides ask.

Opening text for both documents: Honeycomb is a free web app for Georgia Tech undergraduates. It imports Canvas deadlines and Google Calendar, takes quick entries for everything else, and ranks it all into one "Do this next" card. This prototype uses sample data, so do not enter real links or passwords. Log in with the details already filled in.

Scenario 1: Tonight's deadlines

Heavy course load. Covers quick-add, the "Do this next" card and skipping.

It is 7:00 PM on Sunday, Nov 8. You have several deadlines tonight and may not finish them all, so you want to protect your grades. You just remembered a HIST 2111 Reading Quiz that is not in the app yet. It is due tonight at 9:00 PM, should take about 45 minutes, and is worth 3% of your course grade.

Log in and add the quiz. Find out which task to start first and why it ranks first. Find out how long the task after it should take, and which task would cost you the least if you run out of time. Then pretend you finished the top task and mark it done. You aren't up for the task that comes next, so skip it for now and see what the card suggests instead.

Expected result (guide version only): Before the quiz, tonight's 4h 30m of work fits in the 4h 59m left. After it, the list shows 5h 15m of work against 4h 59m left, and ECON 2100 Discussion Post (1%) drops under "Won't fit in time." The quiz moves to the top with 1h 15m to spare. PHYS 2212 Lab Report 7 is next, at 2h 15m. Marking the quiz done moves the clock to 7:45 PM and brings up the lab report. Skipping it brings up CS 1332 Homework 6, and the lab report stays in the list marked "Skipped."

Scenario 2: First-time setup and a wrong date

First-year student. Covers import and editing a due date.

It is Sunday, Nov 8. You are a first-year student opening Honeycomb for the first time. Your classes are in Google Calendar, your deadlines are in Canvas, and you do not want to type assignments in by hand.

Log in and connect your Canvas calendar feed and Google Calendar using the sample data provided. Your instructor just moved MATH 1554 Homework 9 to Friday, Nov 13 at 11:59 PM, but Canvas still shows Wednesday, Nov 11. Correct the date in Honeycomb and confirm that the new date appears and the homework moves in your ranked list.

Expected result (guide version only): Imported tasks appear in one ranked list, tagged "From Canvas," with no % until the student adds one. The homework shows the new date, marked "Changed by you," with "Restore Canvas date." It moves from second to fourth. A bad feed link shows a red error that says how to fix it; no step reaches it, so the guide says where to see it.

Scenario 3: Balancing the week

Club member with a job. Covers the weekly view, cells with progress, and club and class work ranked together.

It is 7:00 PM on Sunday, Nov 8. You are in the robotics club and work shifts at the campus recreation center. You want to see where your whole week stands, including everything outside of class.

Open your week and find your next work shift. See what Honeycomb says to do next, and why. Check how many Clubs tasks are done. Add a task called "Book room for robotics meeting" to Clubs using only a title. Then mark "Order motor parts" done and confirm that the Clubs progress count changes.

Expected result (guide version only): The week shows tasks and fixed blocks by day, starting Sunday, with Next week and Week after tabs. The card shows "Order motor parts" from Clubs ahead of a class task, because it has less spare time. The new task lands in Clubs under "No due date." The Clubs count reads 2 of 4 done, then 2 of 5, then 3 of 5.

Target: under 5 minutes per scenario on a first try, so all three fit in the 25-minute review along with heuristic notes. The outside test uses this as its pass bar.

What gets built

Screens

  • Prototype menu
  • Log in (prefilled demo credentials, working show-password toggle)
  • Connect Canvas and Google Calendar (simulated, with "Where do I find my Canvas feed link?")
  • Home: card, work against time left, ranked list, "Won't fit in time" and "No due date" groups
  • Why this first, with "How ranking works"
  • Add and edit task, with "Restore Canvas date"
  • Week: tasks and fixed blocks, with This week, Next week and Week after
  • Cells overview, showing the cell order, and cell detail

Rules for every screen

  • Every visible control works. If it isn't wired up, it doesn't appear.
  • Every screen has a way back. Marking done, skipping and editing a date can all be undone.
  • Actions show a confirmation. Errors are red and say how to fix them.
  • Progress appears in text. Controls have a visible keyboard focus in ink, because honey on white is only 1.85:1, below WCAG's 3:1 for focus indicators.
  • Text meets 4.5:1 contrast. The vision statement's gold (#A87A00) reaches only 3.85:1 on white, so small text uses a darker shade.
  • It works at phone and laptop widths. Enter saves a form and Esc closes it.

Left out of V1

  • Plan chat, which the research report defers
  • Real imports and real sign-in
  • Reordering cells and late-policy rules
  • Notifications, settings and password reset
